What it does

A self-contained receiver for General Aviation. It hears the traffic around you, works out where you are, and hands both to your electronic flight bag.

ADS-B traffic

Receives and decodes 1090 MHz Mode S and ADS-B, tracking position, altitude, callsign and vertical rate for aircraft around you.

Straight to your EFB

Broadcasts GDL90 over its own WiFi network. SkyDemon and other flight apps pick it up with no cables and nothing to configure.

Position and altitude

A built-in GNSS receiver and barometer give your own position and pressure altitude, so traffic is shown relative to you.

Battery powered

Runs from an internal cell with USB-C charging and a fuel gauge. Nothing to wire into the aircraft.

868 MHz conspicuity in development

Hardware is fitted for FLARM, OGN, PilotAware and FANET reception. The receiver is present; decoding is still being built.

Status at a glance

One status light answers "is it working?", and a built-in web page shows traffic, signal quality and settings from any phone.

Install firmware

Firmware is installed straight from this page over USB — nothing to download and no tools to set up.

  1. Connect KestrelOne to this computer with a USB-C cable.
  2. Press Install and choose the KestrelOne serial port when asked.
  3. Wait for it to finish, then reconnect to the KestrelOne WiFi network.
This browser cannot talk to USB devices. Use Chrome or Edge on a desktop computer — Safari and Firefox do not support it, and neither do phones or tablets. Serial access was blocked. This page must be opened over HTTPS.

Choosing Erase device during install clears saved settings and WiFi credentials as well as the firmware. Leave it unticked to keep them.

Advisory use only. KestrelOne shows traffic that is broadcasting its position. It cannot see aircraft that are not transmitting, and it is not a collision avoidance system. It does not replace looking out of the window.
