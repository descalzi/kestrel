It receives. It does not transmit.

KestrelOne shows you aircraft that are broadcasting their position. It does not make you visible to anyone else — your transponder does that, and nothing here changes it.

It also cannot see aircraft that transmit nothing at all, which on a quiet afternoon is a good many of them. Treat it as another pair of eyes, never as a reason to spend less time looking out of the window.

How it works

Three things, in a box that fits in a flight bag and runs on its own battery.

  1. Listens Picks up 1090 MHz ADS-B and Mode S from aircraft around you and decodes position, altitude, callsign and climb rate.
  2. Works out where you are Its own GNSS receiver and barometer establish your position and pressure altitude, so traffic is shown relative to you rather than in the abstract.
  3. Sends it to your tablet Broadcasts GDL90 over its own WiFi network. SkyDemon and other flight apps pick it up with no cables and nothing to configure.

What you get

No installation, no wiring, no subscription. Charge it, switch it on, connect your tablet.

ADS-B and Mode S

Decodes aircraft broadcasting on 1090 MHz. Those reporting a position appear on the map; those heard without one are listed separately rather than guessed at.

Straight to your EFB

Standard GDL90 over WiFi, the same format commercial receivers use. If your app takes traffic from a portable receiver, it will take this.

Knows where you are

Built-in GNSS and a barometer give your own position and pressure altitude, so nearby traffic is shown as height above or below you.

Grab and go

Internal battery with USB-C charging and a fuel gauge. Nothing to wire into the aircraft, nothing to certify, moves between aircraft freely.

Gliders and paragliders in development

An 868 MHz receiver is fitted for FLARM, OGN, PilotAware and FANET — the traffic that never appears on ADS-B. The hardware is in every unit; the decoding is still being written.

Tells you when it isn't working

One status light answers "is it working?" at a glance, and a built-in web page shows traffic, signal quality and settings from any phone.

Install firmware

Firmware installs straight from this page over USB. Nothing to download, no tools to set up.

  1. ConnectPlug KestrelOne into this computer with a USB-C cable.
  2. InstallPress the button below and choose the KestrelOne serial port.
  3. Connect to itWhen it finishes, join the KestrelOne WiFi network from your phone or tablet.
This browser cannot talk to USB devices. Use Chrome or Edge on a desktop computer — Safari and Firefox do not support it, and nor do phones or tablets. Serial access was blocked. This page must be opened over HTTPS.

Ticking Erase device during install clears saved settings and WiFi credentials along with the firmware. Leave it unticked to keep them.
