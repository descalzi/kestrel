What you get Firmware

What it hears

Airliners and ADS-B-equipped aircraft today, with the gliding and paragliding world arriving as the decoders are finished.

  • ADS-B Position, altitude, callsign and climb rate on 1090 MHz
  • Mode S Transponder replies from aircraft without ADS-B
  • FLARM & OGN soon Gliders, tugs and the Open Glider Network
  • PilotAware soon The P3I network, widely flown in UK GA
  • FANET soon Paragliders and hang gliders
  • ADS-L soon The open EASA-backed standard

KestrelOne is a receiver, so it works alongside your transponder rather than replacing it — that is still what makes you visible to others. It shows what it can hear, which is never quite everything, so it belongs beside a good lookout rather than instead of one.

What you get

Charge it, switch it on, connect your tablet. No installation, no wiring, no subscription.

Straight to your EFB

Traffic goes out as standard GDL90 over WiFi — the format SkyDemon, ForeFlight and other flight apps already expect from a traffic receiver. Join its network and the targets appear on the map you were flying with anyway.

Knows where you are

A multi-constellation receiver fixes your position from GPS, GLONASS and Galileo together, and a barometer reads your pressure altitude. Nearby traffic is shown as height above or below you rather than as a number to work out.

Grab it and go

Runs from its own battery and charges over USB-C. Nothing wired into the aircraft, nothing to certify, and it moves between aircraft as easily as your headset.

Firmware updates

Your KestrelOne arrives ready to fly. When a new version is released it updates straight from your browser over USB — nothing to download, no tools to install.

Latest version Checking…

Your device shows the version it is running on its own web page, under Status. If they match, there is nothing to do.

Open the update page

An update keeps your settings and WiFi credentials. The update page has the full instructions, the release notes and troubleshooting.
